## 1. The build is always red

The only failing job is "Governance manifest covers the spec pin". It fails by design: the
specification was revised three times after the trustees signed it, and no agent may sign for
them. `docs/GOVERNANCE_RESIGNING_REQUEST.md` says exactly what is needed, and was checked again:
two of the three trustees each sign the 64 hex characters of the specification's digest
(`a8177b25…f37329`) with their own key and send back the signature and its time. Nothing else is
needed to close it.

Done: the workflow has a job "Everything except the trustees' signature", which is green when
every other check passed, so a red build no longer hides a second failure.

Open, yours:
- the two signatures;
- whether the second specification (`specifications/discovery_checks_v1.json`, the thresholds
  that decide whether a found law is reported) should be under the trustees' signature too. Today
  it is pinned by hash in code only;
- branch protection on `master` and `stage`, which is a setting of the repository and was found
  off by the outside audit.

## 2. Generated data only, and the product's limits

Until 24 November every recorded result was on laws the project generated. Done since:

- Three published tables were read in development (Kepler's 8 planets, Langmuir's 10 rows,
  Leavitt's 26 Cepheids). The product refuses the two smallest (it needs 24 rows).
- A reading for small tables was built (`apedp_dyn/discovery_l2/small_table.py`). On 120 generated
  tables of 8 to 30 rows it reported 17 laws, none wrong, where the route in force reported none.
  A first version reported 3 wrong laws of 31, all on tables with four rows for each fitted
  constant; a rule of five rows a constant removed them. That rule was chosen on those tables.
- A blind test, PREREG-REAL-TABLES-2026-11, was frozen after two independent reviews and a
  rehearsal, and run once: six held-back tables with known laws (each beside a decoy), pipe
  friction and the expansion rate against the published formulas, 360 generated tables and 360
  traps. Its record is `preregistration/RESULT-REAL-TABLES-2026-11.md`. The reading gave the known
  form on 3 of 5 real tables and 2 counted (3 were needed), so it is **not proposed**. The third
  hung on the judgement of one law with a spurious constant. It reported no law on any real table.
  On generated tables and traps 2 of 44 and 2 of 41 reports were wrong, which passes the 5 % line
  with no margin; all four fail only beyond their own table's rows. The eight tables are used up.

Still true: the route in force was measured with at most three inputs and noise up to 5 %; a law
with a pole inside the range can pass the last checks; the product's reading above 24 rows is
unchanged.

## 3. Results depend on how fast the machine is

Two clocks decide what is tried.

**The memory of confirmed laws** stops its growth after 5 seconds and its remembered whole laws
after 15. Measured on 60 development tables (`benchmarks/small_tables/dev_counted_memory.json`):
the growth was stopped by its clock on 57 of them, and on 30 the second clock ran out as well.
With both clocks halved, as on a machine half as fast, the memory proposed other laws on 23 of 60
tables, and had the true form among its proposals on 10 tables against 11.

Done: `apedp_dyn/proposers/structure_memory_counted.py` counts fits instead of seconds. It
proposed the same laws twice on all 60 tables, had the true form on 10 against the timed
memory's 11, and took about the same time. It is not in force; the timed memory is named in three
frozen plans. Putting it in the reading is yours, and should be compared on fresh tables first.

**The search engines** run for a number of seconds (90 in the runs of this month). That is not
changed and not measured here: a slower machine searches less. A budget in iterations would
remove it and would have to be measured against the recorded runs.

## 12. Runs with the searches could stop for ever

Found on 24 November. The first recursion run with the searches on produced nothing: all six jobs
stopped during a search stage, after 1 to 7 searches, and were ended by their five-hour limits
(`benchmarks/recursion/dev_search_run1_stalled.json`). An earlier development run had gone silent
the same way.

The cause, as far as the logs show: after a search, the portfolio tells its four search processes
to stop and waits for each to go. A process told to stop while Julia's exit hook is running can
wait for ever; the portfolio then waits for it for ever. One such process was told to stop at
15:48 and was still there at 21:01.

The wider-conditions run, which calls the product's own reading, stopped the same way in all four
shards. Across the two runs 10 of 27 searches stopped their job for ever.

Done: `apedp_dyn/proposers/hard_exit.py`. A search process leaves by `os._exit` before Julia's
exit hook, once its answer is handed over. The searches themselves are unchanged. The recursion
runs use it, and with it the first job to finish ran 24 searches in a row with none stopping. The
blind test on real tables was built, before the cause was known, to run each search stage in a
process of its own with a hard limit, so there a stop costs one part: it cost three of the route's
runs their answer.

Open, yours: the product's search on request uses the frozen stage and can stop the same way.
`APEDP_SEARCH_PROCESSES=leave_hard` turns the fix on for the product's reading; without it nothing
changes. The portfolio and the stage are frozen by eight earlier plans and are not edited; making
the new stage the default is a change to what is in force.
