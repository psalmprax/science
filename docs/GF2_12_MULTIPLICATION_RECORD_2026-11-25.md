# What the published record for multiplication in GF(2^12) is

25 November 2026. The owner's fifth decision of that day: establish what the "published 51" is
before anything is built on it. Development; nothing here is in force and nothing is announced.

## The answer

1. **The 51 is the Toffoli count of one circuit, not the best known number of products.** It is in
   Table 7 of Khattar, Shutty, Gidney, Zalcman, Yosri, Maslov, Babbush and Jordan, *Verifiable
   Quantum Advantage via Optimized DQI Circuits*, arXiv:2510.10967 (October 2025; accepted by
   Phys. Rev. X in November 2026). The row for m = 12 reads 51 Toffolis and 1,506 CNOTs, or 51
   parity-controlled Toffolis and no CNOTs. The circuit comes from the synthesis method of Yosri,
   Gavinsky and Maslov, arXiv:2511.20618: a modified Karatsuba multiplication with local
   optimisations. The paper does not call the count optimal.
2. **The number of products has a lower published bound: 42.** The survey of Ballet, Pieltant,
   Rambaud, Randriambololona, Rolland and Chaumine (Russian Math. Surveys 76:1, 2021;
   arXiv:1906.07456), Table 2, gives the best known upper bound on the symmetric bilinear
   complexity of multiplication in GF(2^12) over GF(2) as 42. The same table gives 33 for n = 10
   and 39 for n = 11, where the circuit paper's table has 39 and 47.
3. **42 is now in the repository, explicit and checked exactly**
   (`apedp_dyn/quantum/tower_reference.py`), with a circuit of 42 Toffolis on the three registers
   alone.
4. **So "fewer than 51" is not a discovery.** It is met by mathematics that was in print before
   the circuit. A new result would be 41 products or fewer.

## What was built

GF(2^12) has degree 4 over GF(8). The product of two polynomials of degree at most 3 over GF(8)
has 7 coefficients, so its values at 7 points fix it, and GF(8) has 8. Each value is one
multiplication in GF(8), which takes 6 products of bits. 7 × 6 = 42. The repository's copy of
GF(8) is generated by `u^585`.

Checked, and fixed by tests (`tests/test_tower_reference.py`) and by a record that `--check`
rebuilds (`benchmarks/gf2_multiplication/gf2_mul_tower_42.json`):

* all 1,728 entries of the multiplication tensor are reproduced; none differs;
* the output weights are what the repository's other solver finds from the products alone;
* the 42 products are independent, and none can be dropped alone;
* multiplication from the products is right on 4,096 random pairs;
* the circuit, read from its gates alone, computes `(x, y, z) -> (x, y, z + x*y)` and leaves every
  register as it began, which settles all 2^36 basis states; run bit by bit it is right on 20,000
  random states. The check refuses a circuit with one gate removed.

| Multiplier | Toffolis | CNOTs | Qubits beyond x, y, z |
|---|---|---|---|
| Karatsuba, the benchmark's reference, through the builder here | 63 | 483 | 0 |
| The repository's 54, through the builder here | 54 | 456 | 0 |
| Published (arXiv:2510.10967, Table 7) | 51 | 1,506 | 0 |
| Tower over GF(8), here | 42 | 474 | 0 |

The CNOT counts here are not minimised, and the published circuit was tuned for Toffolis first.

## What this does not show

* **That 42 is the least.** The least is not known. It is at least 31 by the classical argument
  (the products' forms make a binary code of dimension 12 and distance 12) with the Griesmer
  bound; that figure is this repository's arithmetic, not a quoted one, and tables of best codes
  would sharpen it.
* **That nothing below 42 is published.** The survey is from 2021. A search found nothing newer
  for n = 12 (arXiv:2603.07280, March 2026, treats n = 6, 7, 8). A search is not a proof of
  absence, and the survey's table was read through an automated reader: whoever cites it should
  look at the page.
* **Anything about the decoder benchmark.** The circuit has not been put into the DQI decoder.
  The repository's own measured model (one Toffoli a product a call, 10,510 multiplications a
  shot in the six-lever arm) reads 1,325,230 mean Toffolis at 63 products, 1,230,640 at 54
  (measured), and **1,104,520 at 42 (arithmetic, about 16.7 % below 63)**; the ratio to the
  reference would go from 4.32 to 5.18. The controlled call, the window the decoder's
  multiplication writes, and a receipt from the pinned build are all outside that arithmetic.
* **A quantum-circuit record.** The synthesis paper knows that another family of methods needs
  fewer Toffolis at large sizes (it cites Kim et al., ePrint 2022/1095) and notes that family's
  large CNOT count. Whether anyone has published a 42-Toffoli circuit for this field was not
  established.
